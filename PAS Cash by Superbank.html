PAS Cash by Superbank: Empowering Financial Flexibility

Designing an embedded lending experience with flexible repayment and clearer borrowing costs.

Role UX Designer
Timeline Q1 2024
Team / research 1 UX Writer · 2 UX Designers · 2 Project Managers

The challenge

PAS Cash (Pinjaman Atur Sendiri) is an unsecured digital loan product developed by Superbank and integrated into ecosystem apps including Grab and OVO. The project addressed rigid repayment schedules, complex applications, and limited fee transparency.

Designing for different needs

The work considered gig economy workers with variable income, micro-merchants managing daily cash flow, and digital-native professionals who can face mid-month shortfalls. Their needs informed mobile-first interactions, visible balances, and repayment schedules that can match income timing.

Contextual access

New-to-bank users can discover PAS during onboarding and choose to opt in then or later. A home-screen widget and contextual entry points in partner apps make the offer easier to find without relying on intrusive pop-ups. The language emphasizes flexibility and clear costs.

Borrow with control and clarity

An interactive slider lets users choose a loan amount and preferred repayment date, while daily interest updates as the amount changes. Before confirmation, a structured cost summary distinguishes the disbursed amount from total repayment, with contextual explanations for terms.

Repayment support

The dashboard prioritizes remaining balance and the next payment. Dynamic reminder cards appear closer to the due date, and users can repay individual loans early according to their preferences.

Reported impact

The project brief reports a 2.10% gross NPL in 2025, down from 3.80% in 2023; 4 million+ active users across PAS and the GROVO ecosystem integration; and Rp6.2 trillion in gross loan disbursements, up from Rp2.92 trillion.

What I learned

Showing costs upfront can build trust, while too many financial figures can overwhelm users. Contextual placement and flexible repayment can support both access and risk reduction; transparency still needs a clear visual hierarchy.

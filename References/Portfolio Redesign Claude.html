FintechUX DesignUX Research

OVO Nabung by Superbank

Connecting everyday payments with high-yield savings through a joined-up OVO and Superbank experience.

Role
UX Designer
Timeline
Q4 2024
Team
3 UX Designers · 1 UX Writer · 3 Project Managers
Focus
Savings · Payments · Ecosystem integration
OVO

The opportunity

Everyday e-wallet balances are convenient for spending, but they do not earn interest. Moving savings into another banking app can mean extra KYC, app switching, and manual transfers. OVO Nabung brought a high-yield savings product into the OVO ecosystem, with a 5% p.a. interest rate documented in the project brief.

My contribution

I focused on the experience inside the Superbank app: the savings dashboard, Saku setup and management, QRIS and transfer flows, and transaction history. I also worked with the OVO team on shared terminology, design tokens, error states, and deep links between the two apps.

Designing across two apps

01

Connect the journey

Design handoff and return states so users can move between OVO onboarding and Superbank with clear next steps.

02

Organize savings

Use Saku to separate savings while keeping eligible funds connected to everyday payments.

03

Keep payment context

Make funding-source choices, balances, and transaction outcomes understandable within QRIS and transfer flows.

Outcome

+1M
Registered users after 60 days
60%
Users from OVO Nabung integration
2.3M+
Total OVO Nabung users reported

Project brief also reports 3× offline merchant transaction growth and more than doubled usage across GrabFood, GrabBike, and GrabCar.

Reflection

Ecosystem experiences depend on clear handoffs across products. Post-launch feedback highlighted that some users still needed help understanding how interest and balances worked between OVO and Superbank, making clear value communication an ongoing design need.

FintechUX DesignUX Research

PAS Cash by Superbank

Making digital borrowing more flexible and transparent for people with different income cycles.

Role
UX Designer
Timeline
Q1 2024
Team
1 UX Writer · 2 UX Designers · 2 Project Managers
Product
Unsecured digital lending
PAS

The challenge

PAS Cash (Pinjaman Atur Sendiri) is Superbank’s unsecured digital loan product. The project brief identifies three common pain points: rigid repayment schedules, unclear fees, and lengthy applications. PAS was integrated into partner ecosystems including Grab and OVO.

Designing for control and clarity

01

Choose a repayment date

A loan slider lets users set an amount and preferred repayment date; daily interest is shown as the amount changes.

02

Explain the full cost

A structured summary separates the amount disbursed from total repayment before confirmation.

03

Support repayment over time

Dashboard hierarchy, timely reminder cards, and individual early repayment options help users manage what they owe.

Reported business outcomes

2.10%
Gross NPL in 2025, down from 3.80% in 2023
+4M
Active users across PAS and GROVO integration
Rp6.2T
Gross loan disbursements, up from Rp2.92T

What I learned

Showing fees upfront can build trust, while showing too many figures at once can overwhelm people. The design challenge is to preserve full transparency while making the next decision easy to understand.

FintechUI/UXConcept Proposal

BTN Tunas: Flexible Loan Service for MSME

A concept for flexible financial assistance that supports business growth for micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises in Indonesia.

Project
Concept proposal
Focus
Onboarding · Loan access · Repayment
Research
Qualitative interviews · 5 users
BTN

Overview

BTN (Bank Tabungan Negara) is a state-owned bank that provides funding to individual and business customers. To reach entrepreneurs across Indonesia, BTN proposed a platform for MSMEs to access flexible financial assistance that supports business growth.

Disclaimer: This project was created strictly as a concept proposal.

Business Goals

01

Improve onboarding by removing unnecessary steps and increasing conversion.

02

Provide access to flexible financial solutions.

03

Make fund distribution more efficient.

User Need & Problem

Many users want loans to grow their businesses, but loan providers—especially banks—often require collateral for business loans. Applications may also require in-person visits to physical offices, which are difficult for entrepreneurs to fit around their operations.

How might we offer financial services and loans that are easy to use, avoid burdensome requirements, and adapt to each user’s business performance?

Proposed Direction

The proposed direction was a loan-channeling app with flexible options and insights to help users grow their businesses over the long term. Ideas were generated and narrowed around the problem statement, then prioritized to focus feature development.

Core User Flow

The main experience centers on onboarding, accessing a loan limit, and repaying a loan. Repayment includes flexible repayment and full repayment options to make the service’s flexibility clear to users.

Wireframing & Testing

The flow was translated into wireframes to visualize app touchpoints and validate the experience before high-fidelity design. The wireframe proposal was aligned with stakeholders against business and product goals.

Qualitative interviews included five users aged 18–35, male and female, with experience using financial apps. Participants included financially savvy people, entrepreneurs, and digital nomads.

Testing Results

  • 3 of 5 users were concerned that onboarding took too long, while recognizing this is common in banking and financial apps.
  • 4 of 5 were willing to provide business details but unsure how the bank would use that data.
  • 5 of 5 wanted disbursement broken into steps to reduce confusion while entering details.
  • 3 of 5 understood that paying from the home screen covered only the installment due in that period.
  • 5 of 5 could find their latest transaction easily.
  • 5 of 5 understood that flexible repayment was available from the loan-detail page.

Final Product

After testing and design iterations, the final product was described as meeting stakeholder and user needs.

Lessons Learned

  • Data-driven solutions can help manage stakeholder decisions, make a case for a direction, and clarify business impact.
  • Flexible financial services for MSMEs can help businesses grow and encourage them to seek support from financial-service providers.
  • Too many repayment choices can add complexity. Pairing a limited set of flexible options with guided actions may preserve flexibility while making the experience easier to understand.

Accessible Website to Promote Disability Rights Awareness with Ohana Indonesia

A fresh, inclusive redesign for Indonesia's leading disability rights NGO — built for everyone, including the people they advocate for.

Role
UI/UX & Web Designer
Year
2024
Duration
3 months
Tools
Figma · Framer · Lighthouse
OI

Overview

Ohana Indonesia is a non-governmental organization dedicated to advocating for disability rights. As a long-time client, they needed a modern redesign that reflected their mission while dramatically improving accessibility — for their staff, their community, and the people they serve.

Accessibility isn't a feature — it's a foundation. Every design decision was evaluated through the lens of WCAG 2.1 AA compliance and real user impact.

Impact

AA
WCAG Compliant
94
Lighthouse Score
2.4×
Session Duration

Process

01

Accessibility Audit

Ran a full WCAG audit of the existing site. Found 47 critical issues including contrast failures, missing alt text, and keyboard-inaccessible navigation.

02

Co-design with Users

Worked with 6 participants across visual, motor, and cognitive disability groups to co-design navigation patterns and content hierarchy.

03

Design & Build

Created an accessible design system in Figma — high-contrast tokens, scalable type, focus states — then implemented it in Framer with semantic HTML.
